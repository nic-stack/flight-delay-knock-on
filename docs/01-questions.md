# Framing the question

## The brief

> "Our delays keep cascading through the day, and every report just says 'late aircraft'. How much of our delay is actually in our control, and where does it start?"
> — Director of Operations, a U.S. airline (illustrative scenario; the data is real)

Data: Bureau of Transportation Statistics, Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance, October 2025 to September 2026 (12 months, all reporting U.S. airlines).

## Guiding questions

1. **How big is the problem?** What share of flights arrive 15+ minutes late, and how many delay minutes is that, by airline?
2. **What causes it?** How are delay minutes split across the five BTS causes (carrier, late aircraft, National Airspace System, weather, security)?
3. **Where does "late aircraft" delay come from?** Following each aircraft through its day, what started the chain: a carrier delay, weather, the airspace system, or an earlier late-aircraft delay?
4. **Where do chains start?** Which times of day, airports and routes start the most delay that later spreads?
5. **What would help?** Where would extra buffer time, or fixing the first delay, prevent the most downstream minutes?

## Scope decisions

- **Airlines:** all reporting airlines, compared with each other (agreed). Cancelled and diverted flights are counted separately.
- **"Controllable":** carrier delay plus the part of late-aircraft delay that traces back to a carrier delay. The BTS definition alone counts only carrier delay (agreed).
- **Not covered:** costs in dollars, passenger connections, and anything airlines don't report to BTS.
